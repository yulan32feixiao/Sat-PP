One way that young orangutans acquire foraging skills is through a behavior scientists
call peering—closely watching older orangutans as they engage in an activity that the
young have not yet mastered. Since male orangutans typically leave the area of their
birth upon maturity and females do not, Beatrice Enmann and her colleagues
hypothesized that it is more advantageous for immature males than females to devote
attention to orangutans who are immigrants to the home region of the immature
individual, and this should be reflected in sex-specific differences in peering behavior.
