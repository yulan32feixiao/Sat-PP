This passage is from Charles A. Eastman (Ohiyesa), The Indian Today: The Past and
Future of The First American, originally published in 1915.

It was not, then, wholly from ignorance or improvidence that he failed to establish
permanent towns and to develop a material civilization. To the untutored sage, the
concentration of population was the prolific mother of all evils, moral no less than
physical. He argued that food is good, while surfeit kills; that love is good, but lust
destroys; and not less dreaded than the pestilence following upon crowded and
unsanitary dwellings was the loss of spiritual power inseparable from too close contact
with one’s fellow men.
